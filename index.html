  • Open Knowledge: Sharing techniques and discoveries globally

Underwater Launch System

A revolutionary two-stage propulsion system that begins at the ocean floor.

How It Works

Preparation Phase

The spacecraft is positioned at the ocean floor with laser heating systems targeting the water chamber. The deep ocean provides:

  • Natural pressure containment for the heating process
  • Abundant water for conversion to superheated steam
  • Heat sink for the intense thermal energy generated

Stage 1: Steam Propulsion

When optimal temperature and pressure are reached, the craft is released from its moorings. The pressurized steam provides:

  • Initial thrust to break the water surface
  • Continued acceleration to reach minimum altitude of 3,000+ feet
  • At apex, the stage 1 components detach, significantly reducing weight

Stage 2: Salt Combustion

The craft continues with its second propulsion system:

  • A separate chamber containing pre-heated combustible salts ignites
  • Provides high-energy thrust for orbital insertion
  • Clean-burning with minimal environmental impact

Alternative Systems

Explore unconventional approaches to spacecraft systems with multiple backup options.

Energy Sources

  • Modified photovoltaic arrays from consumer solar panels
  • Thermal generators using waste heat
  • Mechanical energy storage using recycled flywheels
  • Biological fuel cells using waste material
  • Radioisotope generators from smoke detector components

Life Support

  • Algae-based oxygen generation systems
  • Water recycling from modified home filtration
  • Food production using vertical farming techniques
  • Waste processing with mycelium decomposition
  • Radiation shielding using water and organic material layering

Propulsion Alternatives

  • Hybrid rockets using paraffin and liquid oxidizers
  • Air-breathing rocket stages for lower atmosphere
  • Electromagnetic tethers for orbital maneuvers
  • Solar thermal propulsion for deep space
  • Modified automotive engines for first-stage propulsion
  • Underwater steam launch with salt combustion second stage

Navigation & Control

  • Smartphone inertial sensors for attitude determination
  • Modified webcams for star tracking
  • Distributed computing systems using multiple microcontrollers
  • Radio direction finding for positioning
  • Manual backup systems using mechanical computers
